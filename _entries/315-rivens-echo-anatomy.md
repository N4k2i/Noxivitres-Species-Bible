Echo anatomy is Riven’s term for temporary founder-authored structures that resemble real body parts closely enough to move and feel useful but are not committed as permanent anatomy. They let his Origin Glassheart experiment without changing the stable six-arm body plan every time a new idea appears.

- Echo limbs can copy an existing arm, hand, tail segment, plate, or sensor structure.

- They are built mostly from dense Vitrite scaffolds and temporary BlackGlass.

- They have sensation, but usually less nuanced sensation than permanent anatomy.

- They dissolve when the experiment ends or resources are needed elsewhere.

- An echo can become permanent only through a separate full integration process.

### **DEEPER CANON**

An echo hand might appear beside a permanent hand while Riven tests a different reach angle. A temporary support limb might brace a heavy object without requiring a seventh permanent shoulder socket. A sensory shard might orbit near the head for a few minutes while the core checks whether the data is useful enough to justify keeping a new sensor.

Echo anatomy is not holographic. It has mass, can touch things, can be damaged, and costs material. The temporary nature comes from construction strategy: the body deliberately avoids investing in all the deep long-term tissues and structural interfaces required for a permanent organ.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Vitrites form a dense framework first, then coat it with enough BlackGlass and biological-compatible material to provide the intended function. Because the scaffold is designed for removal, the core keeps clean dissolution paths so the temporary structure can be reclaimed without tearing surrounding anatomy.

The echo inherits Riven's visual language where practical. A copied hand should resemble his hand, not a stock black claw, unless the point of the experiment is specifically to test a different morphology.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Echo anatomy is resource-expensive and coordination-heavy. Producing many echoes at once can make permanent limbs feel sluggish because the nervous system is processing too many new endpoints.

Temporary structures are also less durable. They are excellent for experimentation and situational adaptation, not a free way to maintain fifty combat-ready arms forever.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Use echoes for temporary dramatic forms without permanently escalating the reference sheet.

- Keep their construction visibly Noxivitre through nanite scaffolding.

- Let a successful echo occasionally become the prototype for a later permanent trait.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
A good version of **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** feels specific to the person wearing it. The system can be technically complicated without making the character talk like a manual.

The useful questions here are **Generation-Zero core architecture, founder-only body-map authoring, owner canon, fatigue limits, and the difference between precedent and a public trait list**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Riven’s Echo Anatomy**, practical details can include clothing, movement, sleep, and ordinary social interaction. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include reduced precision, extra heat or energy use, and temporary fallback behavior. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include a more compact resting state, subtle BlackGlass seams, and visible nanite traffic under load. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through clothing, privacy, and ordinary social interaction—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Riven’s Echo Anatomy**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Founder capability still has costs. Riven and Blade can author pathways that later Noxivitres normally cannot, but fatigue, risk, preference, and owner canon still matter. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Questions worth answering

- What does **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** feel like when nothing is wrong?
- Which parts of **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** are automatic, and which can be deliberately controlled?
- Does **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** behave differently during a Glassshift, active repair, or emotional stress?
- What misconception about **Riven’s Echo Anatomy** would the character be tired of correcting?

#### Character-facing note

**Riven’s Echo Anatomy** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
