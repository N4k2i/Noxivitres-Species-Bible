Riven and Blade should look like two specific people who became Noxivitres, not two examples generated from one species template. The founder forms share structural language while preserving different silhouettes, colors, inherited traits, and mutation biases.

- Riven keeps his own established palette and preexisting unusual anatomy.

- Blade keeps his own established palette and former Common baseline identity.

- Both use Fractureface logic without identical plate arrangements.

- Both can use orbital BlackGlass without identical armor layouts.

- Six-arm anatomy should be integrated realistically rather than pasted onto the sides of the torso.

### **DEEPER CANON**

The easiest visual failure is overusing black crystalline material until the founders become indistinguishable except for accent color. BlackGlass should appear as seams, structural regions, mobile plates, channels, temporary scaffolds, and mutable surfaces while leaving enough fur, plating, markings, and personal shape for the original character to remain obvious.

The second failure is treating Noxivitre as 'Protogen with more parts.' A mature founder reference should make the face architecture, floating systems, plate behavior, and core-driven anatomy clearly different even before somebody counts the arms.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Nanite effects should have purpose. A particle trail can indicate a stressed bridge, active authoring, rapid plate movement, or repair. Constant maximum-density particles everywhere make it impossible to tell when the system is actually doing more work.

Fractureface displays should remain expressive enough that the species still feels character-focused rather than hiding everyone behind unreadable armor.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

A redesign that changes every recognizable feature at once should be treated cautiously unless the relevant owner wants that degree of transformation. Continuing mutation is a tool for development, not an obligation to erase the original OC.

Conversely, keeping every Protogen armor convention forever undermines the point that they became a new species.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- For future art, check silhouette, face architecture, six-arm socket logic, floating structures, and personal identity separately.

- Use BlackGlass selectively so fur and old design language still matter.

- Let Riven and Blade diverge more as mature forms rather than converging into one model.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
For **Founder Visual Identity Rules**, the species rules should answer the practical questions first and leave spectacle for when the story actually needs it.

The useful questions here are **Generation-Zero core architecture, founder-only body-map authoring, owner canon, fatigue limits, and the difference between precedent and a public trait list**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Founder Visual Identity Rules**, practical details can include movement, clothing, sleep, and ordinary social interaction. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Founder Visual Identity Rules** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include slower response, extra heat or energy use, and temporary fallback behavior. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Founder Visual Identity Rules** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include subtle BlackGlass seams, visible nanite traffic under load, and a more compact resting state. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through movement, ordinary social interaction, and clothing—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Founder Visual Identity Rules**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Founder capability still has costs. Riven and Blade can author pathways that later Noxivitres normally cannot, but fatigue, risk, preference, and owner canon still matter. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Questions worth answering

- What is the first small sign that **Founder Visual Identity Rules** is overloaded, damaged, or badly calibrated?
- Does **Founder Visual Identity Rules** behave differently during a Glassshift, active repair, or emotional stress?
- What does **Founder Visual Identity Rules** feel like when nothing is wrong?
- Which parts of **Founder Visual Identity Rules** are automatic, and which can be deliberately controlled?

#### Character-facing note

**Founder Visual Identity Rules** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
