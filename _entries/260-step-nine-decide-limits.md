Step Nine: Decide Limits is a creator-facing rule meant to make character design easier. The goal is to give enough structure that characters read as the same species while leaving enough freedom that two creators can make radically different designs.

- Check how another species might misunderstand it.

- Check how the individual learned to coordinate it.

- Check whether the trait can be voluntarily suppressed.

- Use at least one species-specific visual such as ear or horn structures that integrate with the skull rather than looking glued on.

- Make sure the result still has an actual personality and life outside the body design.

Use this page as a build tool rather than lore that every character has to recite. If a choice does not matter for the character, skip it; if a choice creates a better design question, follow it even if the answer is weird.

A finished character should be explainable in a few sentences even if their body is extremely complicated. Complexity is fine; confusion is not the goal. The BlackGlass logic should make the strange parts feel connected to each other.

This is the normal version of **Step Nine: Decide Limits**, not a ban on future exceptions. If a later character breaks the default, document what changed, what supports the exception, and whether the change is personal or actually updates species-wide canon.

### **DEEPER CANON**

For Noxivitres, step nine: decide limits is treated as a real system rather than a decorative trait. It connects to character-design decisions, readability, rarity classification, personal history, and the minimum rules that make an OC feel like a Noxivitre. The working substrate is the chosen body base, BlackGlass pattern, Vitrite behavior, mutation history, limits, personality, and visual construction language. That means the feature should have a readable input, a physical or informational pathway, and a stable result; BlackGlass is flexible, but it is not an excuse for effects to happen with no internal reason. When a later writer expands step nine: decide limits, the first question should be what part of the body map is being changed or interpreted, and the second should be what keeps that change stable after the dramatic moment is over.

The main failure state for step nine: decide limits is building a checklist instead of a person, copying a Protogen silhouette too closely, using rarity as a substitute for design, or stacking traits without deciding how any of them function together. Noxivitre biology usually tries to correct inconsistencies instead of immediately collapsing, so small errors can produce compensation: nanites reroute signals, BlackGlass reinforces weak structures, or the Glassheart temporarily limits a mutation until coordination catches up. That correction is not free. It can consume energy, create heat, reduce regeneration elsewhere, slow sensory processing, or force the individual to consciously manage something that would normally be automatic. These consequences are useful because they keep an extremely flexible species from becoming consequence-free while still allowing very strange bodies to function.

- Define the body-map entry for step nine: decide limits before defining flashy secondary effects.

- Decide what keeps the feature stable while the individual is asleep, injured, distracted, or low on nanites.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

BlackGlass does not build **Step Nine: Decide Limits** from a blank species template. It starts with the individual: their proportions, coloration, existing plating or fur, scars, learned movement, and the body map they already recognize as themselves. Vitrite swarms handle the fine integration work while the Glassheart checks whether the result still belongs to that person. That is why copied anatomy normally looks personal rather than generic. If **Step Nine: Decide Limits** is duplicated or rebuilt, details such as a more compact resting state and subtle BlackGlass seams should still fit the owner’s established design language unless corruption or deliberate experimentation is the point of the scene.

Control can be distributed instead of centralized. For step nine: decide limits, the Glassheart sets the high-level body-map state, local BlackGlass handles structural adaptation, and nearby Vitrite swarms perform thousands of small corrections every second. If one layer is interrupted, the others may keep the feature functioning in a degraded state rather than switching it completely off. This gives writers room for partial failures: a floating segment may stay suspended but lose fine sensation, a display may keep working while expression becomes delayed, or a duplicated limb may remain healthy while coordination becomes clumsy. Partial failure is usually more interesting than treating every problem as either perfect function or total shutdown.

- Keep original coloration and personal construction language when BlackGlass duplicates an existing structure unless corruption is specifically part of the story.

- Give partial failures a readable symptom instead of making every malfunction an instant catastrophic shutdown.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

For **Step Nine: Decide Limits**, rarity should be assigned by the whole build rather than by how flashy this one feature looks. Common is the stable/simple end of the scale; Uncommon adds noticeable complexity; Rare permits major structural deviation; Legendary stacks several demanding systems; Mythic is the intentionally extreme open tier. Unobtanium is different because it is creator-locked for founder-class or canon-authority reasons, not because it has the biggest number of limbs. A public character can therefore have an outrageous version of **Step Nine: Decide Limits** and still remain Mythic if the concept does not require reserved founder mechanics.

In everyday life, step nine: decide limits matters through how the character moves, dresses, talks, maintains their body, reacts to mutation, interacts with others, and exists when nothing dramatic is happening. The most convincing Noxivitre designs show at least a few of those consequences even when nothing is going wrong. A practical reference sheet can note whether the design can be explained clearly, drawn consistently, written in motion, and recognized as the same character before and after a Glassshift. None of this has to become constant exposition in a story; it is background logic that lets a character move naturally and lets artists or writers answer small questions consistently. Edge cases are allowed, but an exception should state what rule it breaks, why BlackGlass can support it, and what new limitation or tradeoff appears because of that exception.

- Classify the entire design, not one isolated trait; rarity measures complexity and BlackGlass intensity, not worth or combat power.

- Write at least one mundane consequence of step nine: decide limits so the feature still feels real outside transformation scenes.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Give the trait one practical downside that is not just 'it hurts.'

- Write one example of the trait on a calm character and one on a stressed character.

- Give the feature a visual tell that becomes obvious during a Glassshift.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
**Step Nine: Decide Limits** is one of those details that can look simple on a reference sheet and turn into a dozen small decisions the moment the character starts moving.

The useful questions here are **accessibility, fashion, privacy, regional custom, economics, architecture, and the fact that no single community represents every Noxivitre**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Step Nine: Decide Limits**, practical details can include sleep, clothing, movement, and maintenance. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Step Nine: Decide Limits** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include extra heat or energy use, slower response, and temporary fallback behavior. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Step Nine: Decide Limits** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include subtle BlackGlass seams, a more compact resting state, and visible nanite traffic under load. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through privacy, movement, and ordinary social interaction—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Step Nine: Decide Limits**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Social friction is not biological failure. Communities can solve the same practical problem in different ways. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Questions worth answering

- What is the first small sign that **Step Nine: Decide Limits** is overloaded, damaged, or badly calibrated?
- Does **Step Nine: Decide Limits** behave differently during a Glassshift, active repair, or emotional stress?
- What does **Step Nine: Decide Limits** feel like when nothing is wrong?
- What misconception about **Step Nine: Decide Limits** would the character be tired of correcting?

#### Character-facing note

**Step Nine: Decide Limits** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
