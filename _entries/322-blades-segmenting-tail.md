Blade’s tail develops BlackGlass segmentation as his Noxivitre anatomy matures. The tail remains one continuous personal body part in its default state, but mapped seams allow sections to separate slightly, float in formation, or reconfigure their spacing during specific tasks.

- Default state reads as one tail.

- Segment seams are functional Noxivitre structures.

- Sections can separate within a safe local chain.

- Sensory and motor continuity persist through nanite bridges.

- The tail does not become a swarm of independent drones.

### **DEEPER CANON**

Segmenting solves several practical problems. The tail can curve around furniture without forcing every section into one rigid bend, compact itself behind Blade in a crowded room, or briefly spread its sections to increase balance feedback during difficult movement.

Visually, the effect should preserve the original tail design rather than replacing it with generic floating cubes. Fur, plating, markings, and personal shape continue across the seams so the separated pieces still read as one tail.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Vitrites maintain a chain map between segments. If one segment drifts slightly, neighboring segments adjust spacing to preserve the tail's overall curve. During rapid movement, the system can temporarily stiffen the bridges so the chain behaves more like a conventional tail.

The Origin Glassheart records both compact and separated tail states as normal, reducing the amount of conscious control needed.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

A severed or heavily damaged segment is still an injury. Deliberate segmentation occurs only along mapped seams and within supported distance. The existence of floating gaps does not make every cut safe.

If resources are low, the tail closes its seams and returns to a simpler continuous configuration.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Preserve Blade’s established tail silhouette in the closed state.

- Use segmentation for balance and expression, not only spectacle.

- Keep mapped seams distinct from accidental damage.
