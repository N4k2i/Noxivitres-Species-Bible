Blade’s founder core develops a Mirror Lattice: an Origin Glassheart architecture that is exceptionally good at precise duplication, symmetrical comparison, error detection, and stable temporary segmentation. The name describes its anatomy-processing style, not a supernatural mirror power.

- Strong duplication fidelity.

- Fast left/right and pairwise error comparison.

- Excellent support for segmented structures that must reconnect precisely.

- Can author new anatomy while preserving visual consistency.

- Does not copy other people’s bodies by looking at them.

### **DEEPER CANON**

When Blade grows a new structure from his own template, the Mirror Lattice can compare thousands of corresponding measurements against the existing side of his body. That is why his duplicated arms tend to look uncannily native instead of like rough approximations. The core is matching fur direction, plate thickness, joint range, sensory density, and even small asymmetries it considers part of Blade rather than manufacturing idealized perfect clones.

The lattice can also deliberately break symmetry. If Blade has a scar, marking, or owner-defined asymmetrical feature, the core can preserve it rather than 'correcting' him into a generic mirrored model.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Vitrites run constant pairwise diagnostics. A plate on the left and its rough counterpart on the right do not need to occupy identical positions, but unexpected differences trigger a check for damage or obstruction.

During decoupling and reconnection, the same system verifies that surfaces meet in the correct orientation before tissue or hardware fuses back together.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Mirror logic can become a liability if the core overcorrects during a novel asymmetric injury. In those cases Blade may need to consciously accept a new asymmetry so the body stops trying to equalize it.

The lattice also cannot duplicate unlimited mass from nothing. Copying a structure still requires material and energy.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Use mirror diagnostics to explain Blade’s precise repair behavior.

- Preserve intentional asymmetry as identity, not error.

- Keep the system centered on Blade’s own body map rather than external copying powers.

Useful warning signs for this feature include temporary fallback behavior, extra heat or energy use, and slower response. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.
