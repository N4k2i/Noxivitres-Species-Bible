Riven’s two ear pairs and horns become a dense Noxivitre sensory crown after conversion. The structures remain physically his old anatomy, but BlackGlass adds signal channels that let them cooperate with Fractureface sensors, nanite fields, and spatial mapping.

- Both biological ear pairs remain meaningful rather than decorative duplicates.

- Different ears can specialize in different frequency or directional tasks.

- Horns gain BlackGlass-responsive sensory channels without becoming generic antennae.

- The crown helps map floating anatomy around the body.

- Sensory overload is possible when too many channels are left fully open.

### **DEEPER CANON**

Riven does not hear four completely separate copies of every sound. The brain and Glassheart fuse the input into a spatial model, using tiny timing differences between the ear pairs to determine direction and movement with unusual precision. One pair can prioritize ordinary environmental hearing while the other monitors higher-frequency mechanical noise, nanite activity, or other owner-approved ranges.

His horns add another layer. BlackGlass channels along or inside them can detect field changes, vibration, or the behavior of nearby Vitrites. The horns still look and move according to Riven's design; their new function does not require replacing them with radio towers.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Vitrites dynamically filter the crown. In a quiet room, more channels can stay open. In a loud crowd, the system suppresses redundant input so four ears do not become four times the problem. During a Glassshift, filtering may temporarily weaken because the core is using the same sensory network to verify new anatomy.

The crown also helps coordinate floating legs and tail without constant visual checking. Riven has a persistent sense of where those components are relative to his torso, similar to proprioception but expanded across physical gaps.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Sensory precision is not omniscience. Walls, interference, unfamiliar nanite signatures, extreme noise, and damage can all create blind spots. Too much data can produce headaches, delayed filtering, involuntary ear movement, Fractureface glitches, or a need to reduce active channels.

This limitation keeps the sensory crown useful without turning it into a universal detector.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Assign each ear pair a slightly different practical emphasis if a future reference sheet needs more detail.

- Let horn channel brightness or surface patterns reflect active sensing.

- Use sensory overload as a mundane consequence of an unusually dense sensor layout.
