Community Research Scenario is a seed rather than a fixed storyline. It exists so the setting can grow outward from the same BlackGlass rules without forcing every character into one canon plot.

- Immediate problem: how another species might misunderstand it

- Character problem: whether the trait can be voluntarily suppressed

- BlackGlass complication: how it changes balance and movement

- Escalation should change the body or the interpretation of the body, not just raise the danger level.

- The ending can resolve the event without resolving every mutation it caused.

A strong version of this scenario should make the species rules matter to the plot. Use details such as subtle glitching around active mutations, organic fur interrupted by synthetic structures, or moving surface patterns that react to emotion as evidence of what BlackGlass is doing instead of explaining everything through exposition.

The scenario is deliberately open enough to be horror, drama, comedy, action, or slice-of-life depending on the cast. What makes it species-specific is that the body itself can become part of the consequence and remain changed after the immediate problem is over.

This is the normal version of **Community Research Scenario**, not a ban on future exceptions. If a later character breaks the default, document what changed, what supports the exception, and whether the change is personal or actually updates species-wide canon.

### **DEEPER CANON**

The important thing about community research scenario is that it has to work inside a mutable Noxivitre body, not only look good in a reference sheet. It connects to social meaning, consent, accommodation, community variation, and the ways people build traditions around mutable bodies without requiring one universal culture. The working substrate is families, friend groups, local communities, institutions, art, clothing, medicine, shared terminology, and personal choices. That means the feature should have a readable input, a physical or informational pathway, and a stable result; BlackGlass is flexible, but it is not an excuse for effects to happen with no internal reason. When a later writer expands community research scenario, the first question should be what part of the body map is being changed or interpreted, and the second should be what keeps that change stable after the dramatic moment is over.

The main failure state for community research scenario is turning one community’s habit into a biological law, treating rarity as caste, ignoring bodily autonomy, or assuming every Noxivitre has the same attitude toward mutation. Noxivitre biology usually tries to correct inconsistencies instead of immediately collapsing, so small errors can produce compensation: nanites reroute signals, BlackGlass reinforces weak structures, or the Glassheart temporarily limits a mutation until coordination catches up. That correction is not free. It can consume energy, create heat, reduce regeneration elsewhere, slow sensory processing, or force the individual to consciously manage something that would normally be automatic. These consequences are useful because they keep an extremely flexible species from becoming consequence-free while still allowing very strange bodies to function.

- Define the body-map entry for community research scenario before defining flashy secondary effects.

- Decide what keeps the feature stable while the individual is asleep, injured, distracted, or low on nanites.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

BlackGlass does not build **Community Research Scenario** from a blank species template. It starts with the individual: their proportions, coloration, existing plating or fur, scars, learned movement, and the body map they already recognize as themselves. Vitrite swarms handle the fine integration work while the Glassheart checks whether the result still belongs to that person. That is why copied anatomy normally looks personal rather than generic. If **Community Research Scenario** is duplicated or rebuilt, details such as fur or plating shifting around the attachment points and ear pairs turning toward different sounds should still fit the owner’s established design language unless corruption or deliberate experimentation is the point of the scene.

Control can be distributed instead of centralized. For community research scenario, the Glassheart sets the high-level body-map state, local BlackGlass handles structural adaptation, and nearby Vitrite swarms perform thousands of small corrections every second. If one layer is interrupted, the others may keep the feature functioning in a degraded state rather than switching it completely off. This gives writers room for partial failures: a floating segment may stay suspended but lose fine sensation, a display may keep working while expression becomes delayed, or a duplicated limb may remain healthy while coordination becomes clumsy. Partial failure is usually more interesting than treating every problem as either perfect function or total shutdown.

- Keep original coloration and personal construction language when BlackGlass duplicates an existing structure unless corruption is specifically part of the story.

- Give partial failures a readable symptom instead of making every malfunction an instant catastrophic shutdown.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

For **Community Research Scenario**, rarity should be assigned by the whole build rather than by how flashy this one feature looks. Common is the stable/simple end of the scale; Uncommon adds noticeable complexity; Rare permits major structural deviation; Legendary stacks several demanding systems; Mythic is the intentionally extreme open tier. Unobtanium is different because it is creator-locked for founder-class or canon-authority reasons, not because it has the biggest number of limbs. A public character can therefore have an outrageous version of **Community Research Scenario** and still remain Mythic if the concept does not require reserved founder mechanics.

In everyday life, community research scenario matters through names, clothes, jokes, relationships, celebrations, accessibility, etiquette, art, work, and whether someone treats a Glassshift as exciting, annoying, frightening, or just normal. The most convincing Noxivitre designs show at least a few of those consequences even when nothing is going wrong. A practical reference sheet can note who gets to decide what a trait means, what is considered private, which accommodations become ordinary, and how different communities disagree without breaking canon. None of this has to become constant exposition in a story; it is background logic that lets a character move naturally and lets artists or writers answer small questions consistently. Edge cases are allowed, but an exception should state what rule it breaks, why BlackGlass can support it, and what new limitation or tradeoff appears because of that exception.

- Classify the entire design, not one isolated trait; rarity measures complexity and BlackGlass intensity, not worth or combat power.

- Write at least one mundane consequence of community research scenario so the feature still feels real outside transformation scenes.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Give the feature a visual tell that becomes obvious during a Glassshift.

- Decide what the nanites do when the feature is damaged.

- Design a Common interpretation and a Mythic interpretation of the same idea.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
The best way to understand **Community Research Scenario** is to imagine the character dealing with it on a boring Tuesday, not only during a transformation scene.

The useful questions here are **load paths, range of motion, proprioception, attachment or suspension logic, and the way nearby structures make room for it**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Community Research Scenario**, practical details can include sleeping on one side, keeping multiple ear pairs from colliding, filtering overlapping sound sources, and headphones and headwear. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Community Research Scenario** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include a mechanical ear falling back to basic hearing, one ear pair muting itself to prevent overload, and incorrect direction finding. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Community Research Scenario** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include fur or plating shifting around the attachment points, small calibration flickers near mechanical bases, and ear pairs turning toward different sounds. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through headphones and headwear, filtering overlapping sound sources, and keeping multiple ear pairs from colliding—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Community Research Scenario**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. The safest fallback is usually a mechanically conservative posture or reduced range, not the anatomy vanishing for no reason. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Questions worth answering

- What is the first small sign that **Community Research Scenario** is overloaded, damaged, or badly calibrated?
- What does **Community Research Scenario** feel like when nothing is wrong?
- What misconception about **Community Research Scenario** would the character be tired of correcting?
- Does **Community Research Scenario** behave differently during a Glassshift, active repair, or emotional stress?

#### Character-facing note

**Community Research Scenario** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
