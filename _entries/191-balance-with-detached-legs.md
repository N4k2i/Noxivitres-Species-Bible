When a later writer expands balance with detached legs, the first question should be what part of the body map is being changed or interpreted, and the second should be what keeps that change stable after the dramatic moment is over.

The main failure state for balance with detached legs is sensory overload, phantom signals, delayed proprioception, mismatched expression, pain loops, or too much raw information arriving without useful filtering. That correction is not free. These consequences are useful because they keep an extremely flexible species from becoming consequence-free while still allowing very strange bodies to function.
