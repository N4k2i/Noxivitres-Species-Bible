Canon Submission Template is a seed rather than a fixed storyline. It exists so the setting can grow outward from the same BlackGlass rules without forcing every character into one canon plot.

- Design around how another species might misunderstand it.

- Decide what social assumptions are attached to it.

- Account for whether the body can rebuild it from a stored template.

- Unobtanium should remain special through lore and permission, not by banning ordinary creators from having fun anatomy.

- Mutations should still obey the individual's body map unless the story explicitly establishes corruption or instability.

In practice, this can show up through details like tails with visible mutation history, subtle glitching around active mutations, or moving surface patterns that react to emotion. None of those are mandatory; they are examples of how the same rule can produce different silhouettes while still reading as part of one species.

In-world, **Canon Submission Template** is not something the character has to explain every time it appears. It becomes background life: how plates shift while crouching, heat trapped under armor, access to maintenance seams, and noise when plates touch can all be affected without turning every scene into a biology lecture. The rule is here so those little consequences stay consistent when the story is busy talking about something else.

This is the normal version of **Canon Submission Template**, not a ban on future exceptions. If a later character breaks the default, document what changed, what supports the exception, and whether the change is personal or actually updates species-wide canon.

### **DEEPER CANON**

The important thing about canon submission template is that it has to work inside a mutable Noxivitre body, not only look good in a reference sheet. It connects to story consequences, escalation, evidence, character choices, and reusable canon-building tools. The working substrate is established BlackGlass rules, rarity boundaries, body-map logic, nanite behavior, social context, and whatever local setting the scenario needs. That means the feature should have a readable input, a physical or informational pathway, and a stable result; BlackGlass is flexible, but it is not an excuse for effects to happen with no internal reason. When a later writer expands canon submission template, the first question should be what part of the body map is being changed or interpreted, and the second should be what keeps that change stable after the dramatic moment is over.

The main failure state for canon submission template is a scenario that ignores consequences, gives BlackGlass arbitrary powers for one scene, contradicts established limits without explanation, or exists only to show off a trait. Noxivitre biology usually tries to correct inconsistencies instead of immediately collapsing, so small errors can produce compensation: nanites reroute signals, BlackGlass reinforces weak structures, or the Glassheart temporarily limits a mutation until coordination catches up. That correction is not free. It can consume energy, create heat, reduce regeneration elsewhere, slow sensory processing, or force the individual to consciously manage something that would normally be automatic. These consequences are useful because they keep an extremely flexible species from becoming consequence-free while still allowing very strange bodies to function.

- Define the body-map entry for canon submission template before defining flashy secondary effects.

- Decide what keeps the feature stable while the individual is asleep, injured, distracted, or low on nanites.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

BlackGlass does not build **Canon Submission Template** from a blank species template. It starts with the individual: their proportions, coloration, existing plating or fur, scars, learned movement, and the body map they already recognize as themselves. Vitrite swarms handle the fine integration work while the Glassheart checks whether the result still belongs to that person. That is why copied anatomy normally looks personal rather than generic. If **Canon Submission Template** is duplicated or rebuilt, details such as edges glowing faintly while reseating and plates overlapping more tightly under threat should still fit the owner’s established design language unless corruption or deliberate experimentation is the point of the scene.

Control can be distributed instead of centralized. For canon submission template, the Glassheart sets the high-level body-map state, local BlackGlass handles structural adaptation, and nearby Vitrite swarms perform thousands of small corrections every second. If one layer is interrupted, the others may keep the feature functioning in a degraded state rather than switching it completely off. This gives writers room for partial failures: a floating segment may stay suspended but lose fine sensation, a display may keep working while expression becomes delayed, or a duplicated limb may remain healthy while coordination becomes clumsy. Partial failure is usually more interesting than treating every problem as either perfect function or total shutdown.

- Keep original coloration and personal construction language when BlackGlass duplicates an existing structure unless corruption is specifically part of the story.

- Give partial failures a readable symptom instead of making every malfunction an instant catastrophic shutdown.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

For **Canon Submission Template**, rarity should be assigned by the whole build rather than by how flashy this one feature looks. Common is the stable/simple end of the scale; Uncommon adds noticeable complexity; Rare permits major structural deviation; Legendary stacks several demanding systems; Mythic is the intentionally extreme open tier. Unobtanium is different because it is creator-locked for founder-class or canon-authority reasons, not because it has the biggest number of limbs. A public character can therefore have an outrageous version of **Canon Submission Template** and still remain Mythic if the concept does not require reserved founder mechanics.

In everyday life, canon submission template matters through the aftermath: repairs, conversations, changed routines, new fears or confidence, damaged equipment, relationship shifts, and the boring practical problems that make a wild event feel real. The most convincing Noxivitre designs show at least a few of those consequences even when nothing is going wrong. A practical reference sheet can note what starts the situation, what characters can observe, what choices matter, what changes afterward, and which unanswered question can seed another story. None of this has to become constant exposition in a story; it is background logic that lets a character move naturally and lets artists or writers answer small questions consistently. Edge cases are allowed, but an exception should state what rule it breaks, why BlackGlass can support it, and what new limitation or tradeoff appears because of that exception.

- Classify the entire design, not one isolated trait; rarity measures complexity and BlackGlass intensity, not worth or combat power.

- Write at least one mundane consequence of canon submission template so the feature still feels real outside transformation scenes.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Design a Common interpretation and a Mythic interpretation of the same idea.

- Describe how the feature changes clothing, furniture, movement, or sleeping posture.

- Give the feature a visual tell that becomes obvious during a Glassshift.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
For **Canon Submission Template**, the species rules should answer the practical questions first and leave spectacle for when the story actually needs it.

The useful questions here are **load paths, range of motion, proprioception, attachment or suspension logic, and the way nearby structures make room for it**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Canon Submission Template**, practical details can include noise when plates touch, clothing over raised surfaces, heat trapped under armor, and access to maintenance seams. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Canon Submission Template** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include delayed alignment, one plate staying raised, and edge chipping. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Canon Submission Template** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include repair veins crossing a crack, plates overlapping more tightly under threat, and edges glowing faintly while reseating. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through how plates shift while crouching, access to maintenance seams, and clothing over raised surfaces—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Canon Submission Template**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. The safest fallback is usually a mechanically conservative posture or reduced range, not the anatomy vanishing for no reason. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Questions worth answering

- Which parts of **Canon Submission Template** are automatic, and which can be deliberately controlled?
- How does **Canon Submission Template** affect clothing, furniture, sleep, travel, or personal space?
- What would a Noxivitre medic see about **Canon Submission Template** that an ordinary observer would miss?
- What does **Canon Submission Template** feel like when nothing is wrong?

#### Character-facing note

**Canon Submission Template** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
