Vacuum and Low-Pressure Concepts addresses the practical side of living in a mutable body. Technology and medicine should account for changing dimensions, detached anatomy, nanite compatibility, and the possibility that a patient's current body map is not the one they had last year.

- Design around what emotional states make it more noticeable.

- Decide what clothing or equipment has to change around it.

- Account for how it changes balance and movement.

- Mutations should still obey the individual's body map unless the story explicitly establishes corruption or instability.

- The open tiers should stay genuinely open. Do not quietly move popular traits into Unobtanium just to make them exclusive later.

In practice, this can show up through details like temporary nanite scaffolding, tails with visible mutation history, or secondary sensory structures. None of those are mandatory; they are examples of how the same rule can produce different silhouettes while still reading as part of one species.

In-world, **Vacuum and Low-Pressure Concepts** is not something the character has to explain every time it appears. It becomes background life: sleep, clothing, maintenance, and privacy can all be affected without turning every scene into a biology lecture. The rule is here so those little consequences stay consistent when the story is busy talking about something else.

This is the normal version of **Vacuum and Low-Pressure Concepts**, not a ban on future exceptions. If a later character breaks the default, document what changed, what supports the exception, and whether the change is personal or actually updates species-wide canon.
