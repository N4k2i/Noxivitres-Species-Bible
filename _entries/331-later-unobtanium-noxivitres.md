Riven and Blade are the first Unobtanium Noxivitres, not necessarily the only ones that can ever exist. Future Unobtanium characters can be approved by the creator, but they should not automatically receive copied founder cores or become secret third members of Generation Zero.

- Generation Zero remains Riven and Blade.

- Later Unobtanium characters require explicit creator approval.

- A later Unobtanium can have a different unique core class.

- Founder-only history is not transferable by rarity label.

- Owner-specific concepts should stay with the character they were designed for.

### **DEEPER CANON**

This gives the rarity room to grow. One later Unobtanium might have a core specialized for colony-scale nanite symbiosis; another could maintain impossible-looking phase-separated anatomy; another might carry a setting-specific BlackGlass lineage. None of those concepts need to retcon the founders or pretend they were present at the species' first emergence.

The point of the locked tier is curated canon, not one mandatory super-form.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Later cores can still use ordinary Noxivitre systems—Vitrites, Glasshearts, Fracturefaces, body maps—while adding one or more creator-approved exceptions. That keeps them recognizably the same species.

If a later core interacts with founder resonance, compatibility should be defined explicitly rather than assumed from rarity alone.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Approval to make an Unobtanium Noxivitre does not mean approval to use Riven's Adaptive Lattice, Blade's Mirror Lattice, their exact Origin signatures, or their character lore.

Likewise, losing contact with the creator does not magically convert an approved character into open public property.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Design later Unobtanium around a unique canon idea rather than founder imitation.

- Keep Generation Zero permanently limited to the First Pair unless both owners and creator deliberately rewrite that history.

- Document permissions clearly to avoid ambiguity.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
The short version is useful, but **Later Unobtanium Noxivitres** becomes a lot easier to write once it is treated as part of somebody’s ordinary life.

The useful questions here are **Generation-Zero core architecture, founder-only body-map authoring, owner canon, fatigue limits, and the difference between precedent and a public trait list**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Later Unobtanium Noxivitres**, practical details can include clothing, maintenance, sleep, and privacy. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Later Unobtanium Noxivitres** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include slower response, temporary fallback behavior, and reduced precision. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Later Unobtanium Noxivitres** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include visible nanite traffic under load, subtle BlackGlass seams, and a more compact resting state. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through movement, privacy, and clothing—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Later Unobtanium Noxivitres**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Founder capability still has costs. Riven and Blade can author pathways that later Noxivitres normally cannot, but fatigue, risk, preference, and owner canon still matter. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Character-facing note

**Later Unobtanium Noxivitres** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
