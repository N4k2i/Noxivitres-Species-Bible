Nanites, cyber-organic bodies, and digital expression can remain inspirations, but the silhouette, face architecture, torso design, mutation biology, and rarity logic need their own rules. The goal is recognizable influence without depending on another species' protected identity.

- Design around how it changes balance and movement.

- Decide what happens when the trait is damaged.

- Account for what emotional states make it more noticeable.

- The species can be dramatic without making every member equally powerful. Anatomy, social rarity, and combat capability are separate things.

- The open tiers should stay genuinely open. Do not quietly move popular traits into Unobtanium just to make them exclusive later.

In practice, this can show up through details like visible BlackGlass seams, asymmetrical glass growths, or body segments held together by a nanite bridge. None of those are mandatory; they are examples of how the same rule can produce different silhouettes while still reading as part of one species.

In-world, **Protogen Influence Without Being a Protogen** is not something the character has to explain every time it appears. It becomes background life: clothing, movement, ordinary social interaction, and privacy can all be affected without turning every scene into a biology lecture. The rule is here so those little consequences stay consistent when the story is busy talking about something else.

This is the normal version of **Protogen Influence Without Being a Protogen**, not a ban on future exceptions. If a later character breaks the default, document what changed, what supports the exception, and whether the change is personal or actually updates species-wide canon.

### **DEEPER CANON**

When a later writer expands protogen influence without being a protogen, the first question should be what part of the body map is being changed or interpreted, and the second should be what keeps that change stable after the dramatic moment is over.

The main failure state for protogen influence without being a protogen is a definition that becomes so broad that the species stops being recognizable, or so narrow that people cannot make interesting characters. Noxivitre biology usually tries to correct inconsistencies instead of immediately collapsing, so small errors can produce compensation: nanites reroute signals, BlackGlass reinforces weak structures, or the Glassheart temporarily limits a mutation until coordination catches up. That correction is not free. It can consume energy, create heat, reduce regeneration elsewhere, slow sensory processing, or force the individual to consciously manage something that would normally be automatic. These consequences are useful because they keep an extremely flexible species from becoming consequence-free while still allowing very strange bodies to function.

- Define the body-map entry for protogen influence without being a protogen before defining flashy secondary effects.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

For **Protogen Influence Without Being a Protogen**, rarity should be assigned by the whole build rather than by how flashy this one feature looks. Common is the stable/simple end of the scale; Uncommon adds noticeable complexity; Rare permits major structural deviation; Legendary stacks several demanding systems; Mythic is the intentionally extreme open tier. Unobtanium is different because it is creator-locked for founder-class or canon-authority reasons, not because it has the biggest number of limbs. Riven and Blade also make the species’ ancestry impossible to hide, which is useful rather than embarrassing. BlackGlass stopped functioning like an accessory and became the system that rewrote their body maps, supported detached or duplicated structures, and created a new stable cyber-organic architecture. Noxivitre design should therefore look like an evolutionary or mutagenic divergence from that starting point, not like a Protogen with a new label pasted over it.

- Classify the entire design, not one isolated trait; rarity measures complexity and BlackGlass intensity, not worth or combat power.
