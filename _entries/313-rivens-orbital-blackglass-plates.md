Mature Riven develops BlackGlass plates that are not permanently attached to conventional armor mounts. They occupy mapped orbital zones around the shoulders, upper back, hips, and other owner-approved regions, moving with his posture and clearing space for floating anatomy.

- Orbital plates are body parts, not clothing.

- They can shift several inches or more within mapped zones depending on function.

- They can overlap defensively without becoming indestructible armor.

- Their motion communicates mood and physical readiness.

- Damaged plates can be rebuilt from stored body-map patterns.

### **DEEPER CANON**

The plates solve a real six-arm problem: fixed armor around the ribs and shoulders would constantly obstruct the middle and lower arms. Floating plates can move away from a crossing forearm, settle closer to the torso while resting, or form a temporary protective layer over a vulnerable seam.

Their surfaces carry personal design language rather than a single species-mandated color. Riven's plates should harmonize with his existing palette and markings, with BlackGlass appearing as material behavior rather than a forced recolor.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Each plate maintains a short-range nanite tether and local orientation map. The Glassheart tells it what region of the body it belongs to; local Vitrites handle tiny avoidance movements fast enough that Riven does not consciously dodge his own armor every time he reaches across himself.

A plate can go inert if its local swarm is disrupted, but nearby swarms may catch and hold it before it falls. Reattachment is not necessarily a mechanical click; the mapped orbital position itself is part of the anatomy.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Orbital plates have mass and inertia. Rapidly repositioning several at once costs energy and can throw off movement if done at the wrong time. They also cannot form an airtight invulnerable shell around Riven without sacrificing mobility, sensory access, and nanite flow.

A plate drifting slightly out of normal position can be an early visual sign of fatigue or injury.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Use plate spacing as subtle emotional body language.

- Keep the plate layout asymmetrical enough to feel grown rather than factory-perfect if that fits Riven’s design.

- Let armor movement solve six-arm clearance problems instead of existing only for spectacle.
