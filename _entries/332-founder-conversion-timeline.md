The founder conversion is best treated as a sequence of overlapping thresholds rather than one transformation with a single clean end. This timeline gives writers a shared order without locking every scene to exact dates.

- Stage A — Protogen baseline and dormant/early BlackGlass contamination.

- Stage B — stress-linked mutation and first major body-map rewrites.

- Stage C — repeated arm duplication leading to six permanent arms.

- Stage D — Glassheart stabilization and Noxivitre classification.

- Stage E — mature Fractureface, orbital structures, and founder-specific lattices.

- Stage F — Origin Glassheart authoring and confirmed Unobtanium status.

### **DEEPER CANON**

The useful distinction is between species threshold and rarity maturation. Riven and Blade become Noxivitres once BlackGlass is a permanent self-maintaining body system and their anatomy no longer fits Protogen biology as a complete description. They do not need every mature founder feature on that same day.

Unobtanium emerges later as the Generation-Zero cores prove they can do more than stabilize mutation: they can intentionally establish new body-map branches and retain them as coherent personal anatomy.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

Nanite strains also mature through these stages. Early swarms behave like emergency responders. Mid-conversion swarms learn stable new limb routes. Mature founder Vitrites behave like distributed organs that anticipate maintenance and can participate in authoring.

Archived stage maps remain useful for repair and medical history without forcing the body to revert.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Exact timing can differ between Riven and Blade. Co-origin status does not require every feature to emerge in the same minute or same order.

A story can focus on one stage without needing to explain every later ability immediately.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Use stage labels for chronology instead of inventing contradictory exact dates.

- Allow Riven and Blade to progress at different rates.

- Treat Unobtanium as mature core behavior, not an instant title stamped on the first mutation.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
The short version is useful, but **Founder Conversion Timeline** becomes a lot easier to write once it is treated as part of somebody’s ordinary life.

The useful questions here are **Generation-Zero core architecture, founder-only body-map authoring, owner canon, fatigue limits, and the difference between precedent and a public trait list**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Founder Conversion Timeline**, practical details can include maintenance, movement, privacy, and ordinary social interaction. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Founder Conversion Timeline** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include temporary fallback behavior, slower response, and reduced precision. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Founder Conversion Timeline** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include a more compact resting state, subtle BlackGlass seams, and visible nanite traffic under load. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through maintenance, ordinary social interaction, and sleep—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Founder Conversion Timeline**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Founder capability still has costs. Riven and Blade can author pathways that later Noxivitres normally cannot, but fatigue, risk, preference, and owner canon still matter. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.
