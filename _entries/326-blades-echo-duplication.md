Blade can produce temporary echo structures through the same founder-class authoring principle as Riven, but his version tends to appear as precise short-lived duplicates or segment extensions rather than highly irregular spatial experiments.

- Echo hands or support arms can appear temporarily.

- Temporary plate duplicates can widen protection or brace a structure.

- Echo tail segments can test alternate balance patterns.

- All echoes remain subordinate to the permanent body map.

- Successful echoes can inform later owner-approved permanent changes.

### **DEEPER CANON**

A Blade echo often begins as a near-perfect outline built from dense nanites, then fills with temporary BlackGlass until it can take load. Because his core is so good at duplication, an observer may initially mistake the structure for a permanent new limb. The giveaway is usually the lighter internal construction, visible nanite activity, and the fact that the echo dissolves cleanly after use.

This provides a way to let Blade's body get dramatically complex in a scene without forcing every temporary form onto his permanent reference sheet.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

The Mirror Lattice can reuse known permanent-limb patterns to reduce authoring time, but the nervous system still has to map the extra endpoint. A temporary seventh or eighth arm can therefore exist briefly without implying Blade permanently has eight arms.

Vitrites reclaim most of the scaffold material when an echo dissolves, though some energy is inevitably lost as heat and metabolic waste.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Echo duplication is not an infinite-arm exploit. Each new endpoint consumes coordination bandwidth, and Blade's advantage in symmetry does not eliminate cognitive load.

If permanent anatomy is damaged, the core prioritizes real repair over maintaining optional echoes.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Use echoes to explore possible future forms without committing to them.

- Keep permanent Blade at six arms unless his owner changes that canon.

- Show nanite scaffold differences so readers can distinguish echoes from permanent limbs.

A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Blade’s Echo Duplication** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include extra heat or energy use, slower response, and reduced precision. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Blade’s Echo Duplication** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include subtle BlackGlass seams, visible nanite traffic under load, and a more compact resting state. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through movement, maintenance, and privacy—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Blade’s Echo Duplication**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Founder capability still has costs. Riven and Blade can author pathways that later Noxivitres normally cannot, but fatigue, risk, preference, and owner canon still matter.
