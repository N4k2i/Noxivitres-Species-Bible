Blade’s former Common Protogen body acts as the control case for Generation Zero. It establishes what was not present before conversion and therefore helps separate inherited Protogen features from systems that BlackGlass actually created.

- Original body had a conventional two-arm/two-leg baseline.

- No preexisting floating lower limbs are required in his baseline.

- No second biological ear pair is required in his baseline.

- Later fracture architecture and decoupling are Noxivitre developments.

- His original coloration and personal design remain valid identity anchors.

### **DEEPER CANON**

The value of this baseline is not that Common means boring. It means fewer structural exceptions are needed to explain the starting anatomy. Researchers comparing early scans can see the shoulder count change, new support channels grow through the torso, face architecture separate into movable regions, and BlackGlass plate zones appear where there used to be ordinary fixed construction.

This makes Blade's medical history unusually useful for defining what the species is. Riven shows how Noxivitre BlackGlass can absorb and elaborate an already strange body. Blade shows that it can create the same category of mutable cyber-organic systems from a much simpler starting map.

### **BLACKGLASS & NANITE INTERACTION**

The Origin Glassheart retains archived snapshots of Blade's earlier states, but it does not treat the old two-arm body as the only correct version forever. Once a later form has been stable, coordinated, and identity-compatible long enough, the active body map updates.

That prevents regeneration from constantly trying to delete his newer anatomy and return him to an obsolete Protogen configuration.

### **RARITY, DAILY LIFE & EDGE CASES**

Archived baseline does not mean an instant transformation button back to the past. Reconstructing a much older body state would still require a massive Glassshift, and some mature systems may no longer fit cleanly into it.

The baseline is a historical reference, not a command that Blade must remain visually close to forever.

### **EXPANSION HOOKS**

- Use archived scans to show exactly which features are Noxivitre developments.

- Do not treat Common Protogen ancestry as a weaker personality or lesser origin.

- Keep color and owner-defined design traits as strong identity anchors through every later form.

### Human Notes & Practical Detail
For **Blade’s Common Protogen Baseline**, the species rules should answer the practical questions first and leave spectacle for when the story actually needs it.

The useful questions here are **Generation-Zero core architecture, founder-only body-map authoring, owner canon, fatigue limits, and the difference between precedent and a public trait list**. That is the technical side, but it should show up in ordinary life instead of only in diagrams. With **Blade’s Common Protogen Baseline**, practical details can include privacy, sleep, maintenance, and movement. A character who has had the feature for years usually handles those things without thinking; somebody who acquired it recently may still misjudge space, overcorrect, or consciously plan movements that will eventually become automatic.

Decide what **Blade’s Common Protogen Baseline** feels like from the inside. It might register as ordinary proprioception, pressure, a quiet digital position sense, background diagnostics, or almost nothing until something goes wrong. Useful warning signs for this feature include temporary fallback behavior, reduced precision, and extra heat or energy use. Those signs do not need to mean catastrophic failure. Noxivitre systems usually degrade in layers, so reduced precision or a safer fallback state often appears before anything truly dangerous happens.

For artists and writers, give **Blade’s Common Protogen Baseline** at least one repeatable visual or behavioral tell. Possibilities include subtle BlackGlass seams, a more compact resting state, and visible nanite traffic under load. Keep the tell consistent enough that it feels like the same body from scene to scene. Also test the feature somewhere inconvenient but mundane: a cramped vehicle, an unfamiliar bed, a clinic designed for fixed anatomy, or a crowded hallway. If the design still has a believable answer there—perhaps through maintenance, clothing, and movement—the rule is doing real work instead of existing only for spectacle.

If a character breaks the normal version of **Blade’s Common Protogen Baseline**, write down what is compensating for the exception and what tradeoff appears somewhere else. Founder capability still has costs. Riven and Blade can author pathways that later Noxivitres normally cannot, but fatigue, risk, preference, and owner canon still matter. Rarity should still be judged from the whole build. A visually extreme version can remain inside the open tiers, including Mythic, when it does not use creator-reserved founder authority. Unobtanium is not a reward for having the loudest silhouette.

#### Character-facing note

**Blade’s Common Protogen Baseline** can shape habits, accessibility needs, confidence, embarrassment, fashion, or body language, but it should not replace the character's personality. Noxivitres can have extremely different bodies without those bodies deciding morality, intelligence, social importance, or fighting ability. That separation is important to the open-species side of the canon.
